⏺ No, you don't need a monotone library to make the discovery argument — and in
  one important respect the reset design makes your claim stronger, not weaker.
  Here's the full picture.

  What the current design actually is

  In saturate_stitch (ecd.py:285), every round: all cumulative solutions are
  normalized back to raw primitives, D.reset() wipes the invented list, and
  stitch_core.compress runs fresh over the whole expanded corpus. The comment at
  ecd.py:304–312 records why: stitch names its discoveries fn_0, fn_1, …, which
  collides with the same tokens already in compressed input programs and
  produces self-referential abstractions. So the reset is an engineering
  workaround for stitch's namespace, not a scientific commitment.

  But note what is monotone: sols is cumulative, and the library each round is a
  deterministic function of the solved-so-far corpus. The reachability ratchet
  that DreamCoder's growth exists to provide is functionally preserved —
  abstractions that enabled past solves keep recurring in the corpus, so stitch
  keeps re-finding them, and solved tasks never regress (they're cached). Your
  trajectory artifact's reproducibility (replay cumulative sols → reconstruct
  round-r library) depends on exactly this determinism.

  What a truly growing library would take

  1. Move inventions out of stitch's namespace. Register abstractions as e.g.
  inv_k instead of fn_k. Stitch treats unknown tokens as primitives, so the
  collision documented at ecd.py:304 disappears.
  2. Feed stitch the rewritten corpus, not the normalized one. With inv_k as
  opaque leaves, new abstractions get defined in terms of old ones — that's the
  actual content of "growth."
  3. Drop D.reset(); append. Most of the n_already_invented offset-remapping and
  some of expand_skipped simplifies away, but you'd want a dedupe check for
  alpha-equivalent rediscoveries.
  4. Persist per-round libraries in the run/traj artifacts. A monotone library
  is path-dependent, so corpus_dl.py's replay-to-reconstruct trick stops
  working; you'd save the library snapshot per round instead.
  5. Watch the branching factor. A growing library inflates enumeration; that's
  what DreamCoder's recognition model is for, and you already have dreaming, so
  this is manageable.

  Moderate effort — maybe a day, mostly in the name-mapping machinery and the
  downstream figure scripts that assume replay-determinism.

  Why your argument doesn't need it

  Your "discovered, not hard-coded" claim rests on three legs, none of which is
  library monotonicity: belief is not a primitive (it must be assembled from
  fork/sync or, in phase 2, from the product-category combinators); search
  reaches it with nothing belief-specific in the scheduler (the
  experiment.py:847 comment makes this explicit); and one joint MDL compression
  over the mixed corpus selects it against priced rivals (the mdl_margin
  experiment).

  If anything, reset-and-rederive is the stronger variant of the claim. In
  DreamCoder's incremental scheme, an abstraction added early persists by
  inertia — it never has to re-justify itself. In your scheme, the belief
  constructor must re-earn a top-stitch_iters slot in every round's global
  recompression, from raw primitives, against every rival in the full corpus.
  Full-corpus recompression is also closer to the MDL ideal than DreamCoder's
  greedy incremental step, which is a scale-forced approximation; your corpus is
  small enough that the global optimum is affordable. And your headline library
  is already the final "(B) JOINT COMPRESSION" pass — a one-shot global stitch
  — so the round-by-round library is scaffolding for reachability, not the
  object of the verdict.

  The honest cost of the reset design, which I'd state as a limitation rather
  than hide: nothing guarantees a needed abstraction survives the next round's
  slot competition. You've already lived this — n_obstacle=6 exists precisely so
  the wall policy keeps its stitch slot after belief's fork-wrapped rivals
  enter the pool. A monotone library would relax that corpus-tuning pressure;
  that's the real argument for the change, and it's about robustness, not about
  the validity of the discovery claim.

  For the thesis

  Chapter 3 currently says "inspired by DreamCoder," which is accurate, but I'd
  add one explicit sentence in the ECD-architecture section: the sleep phase
  re-compresses the entire solved corpus from primitives each round rather than
  growing the library incrementally, so each round's library is the MDL-optimal
  one for everything solved so far, and the belief abstraction must re-win its
  place in every round — with the reproducibility of the trajectory figures as a
  side benefit. That turns the discrepancy a careful reader might flag into a
  deliberate, defensible design choice.
