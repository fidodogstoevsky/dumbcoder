  TLDR

  The Q model is not initialized biased in the damaging sense: round 1 runs with
  no model at all (type-uniform prior + a content boost for visible literals),
  the trained model is floored at uniform so it can only speed search up, the
  loop falls back to uniform Q after 2 rounds, and — most importantly — the MDL
  verdict and margin are priced under uniform_type_q, never under the learned
  model. But the Q model's architecture is the single thing a reviewer will
  point at, because its encoder is a hand-built agent detector. Beyond Q, there
  are four other places a reviewer could press, none fatal, all needing explicit
  disclosure or an ablation.

  The Q model, in detail

  What's clean. In experiment.run_phase, the enumeration loop
  (experiment.py:885-944) starts every family on content_q (uniform per type,
  visible-in-frame-0 literals free). The dreamed model only enters via dreamed_q
  (ecd.py:1125-1156), which re-softmaxes per type and then takes max(model, 
  uniform) — the "model can only help" floor — and the loop reverts to
  uniform/content after DREAM_USE_ROUNDS = 2, with an explicit rule that a stall
  on the dreamed Q isn't terminal until a uniform round also stalls
  (experiment.py:963-975). The replay_kinds gate means belief tasks never see
  the dreamed Q until belief has at least one solve, so the model can't steer
  belief's first discovery. And mdl_margin._dl uses uniform_type_q only, so the
  headline margins are independent of anything the network learned. The
  --no-dream flag gives you the full ablation.

  What a reviewer will point at. MatRecognitionModel._encode_one
  (ecd.py:1008-1067) is not a generic grid encoder. It has an innate perceptual
  ontology: it segments the scene into movers (cells vacated between frame 0 and
  the last frame), stationary goals, and walls (value 3 hardcoded); it reserves
  exactly two mover slots and two goal slots, with the docstring saying
  outright this is so "witness/dual belief" scenes are representable; and the
  per-mover trajectory pool is documented as "the key signal for distinguishing
  false-belief tasks." A reviewer can say: the recognition model has a built-in
  agent/goal/obstacle parse of the world — an agency prior in perception,
  engineered after looking at what the belief tasks need.

  The defense you actually have is decent, and worth writing down explicitly:
  (1) every feature is extensional — occupancy statistics of the observed
  frames; nothing encodes a second grid, a divergence between world and
  representation, or any other agent's state, so it's an "animacy detector," not
  intensionality; (2) it only affects search order, with the uniform floor and
  fallback guaranteeing reachability, and (3) the verdict, census, cube test,
  and MDL margin are all Q-independent. But the argument becomes airtight only
  with the ablation: if a --no-dream run still produces the same verdict (just 
  slower), the encoder objection is moot; if the headline run needs dreaming, 
  the objection has real bite. I'd make that ablation a reported result, not
  just a flag. A cheaper variant: swap the hand-built encoder for a generic
  conv/pool encoder and show the verdict is unchanged.

  One small factual point for the thesis text: the network's weights are freshly
  default-initialized every round (dream builds a new MatRecognitionModel each
  call), so there's no weight-level bias to speak of — the bias question is
  entirely architectural.

  Other places a reviewer could press

  1. optimize / neg_distance are teleological primitives. optimize(u, av)
  (dsl.py:112-133) is a BFS-optimal greedy goal-seeker — full rational agency as
  one primitive, with walls (value 3) as blockers baked into the BFS. A
  reviewer can say you've given the system desire and rational action innately,
  and only belief is left to find. Your framing survives this — the claim is
  about intensionality (acting on a private model that diverges from the world),
  and optimize reads only the one visible grid, so it's a world-dynamics
  operator like step — but the thesis must say this explicitly and narrow the
  claim from "purely non-mental basis" to "purely extensional basis": no
  primitive reads or writes a representation of the world distinct from the
  world; the private channel and its divergence are what must be assembled.
  "Non-mental" invites the "but desire is mental" reply; "extensional" is the
  property the code actually has.

  2. The task filters guarantee belief is the unique explanation. Every belief
  generator rejects scenes any enumerated non-mental rival reproduces
  (_physically_explainable, _displaced_goal_explainable,
  _witness_rival_explainable, _dual_rival_explainable,
  _scope_complements_all_fail). Two sub-objections: (a) "you curated the data
  until only belief fits — of course MDL finds belief." The answer is that this
  is exactly Sally-Anne staging: identifiability of the stimulus is a
  precondition of a false-belief test, not the result; the discovery claim is
  that the searcher, prior, and compressor contain no belief notion, and they
  don't. (b) Sharper: the rival batteries are finite, hand-enumerated families —
  maybe an unenumerated short non-mental program explains the scenes. Your real
  defense here is the search itself: under uniform/content Q, solve_enumeration
  is best-first in description length and keeps the shortest solution found
  (length tiebreak at ecd.py:772), so any cheaper extensional program would be
  found before the belief compound. That defense is contingent on timeouts, so
  state it as "no non-mental program at or below the belief program's DL
  reproduces any belief scene, verified by exhaustive-below-budget enumeration"
  if you can afford to verify that claim once, offline, for the final corpus.

  3. Corpus-mix and curriculum tuning toward the result. Two
  disclosed-in-comments choices were made because they help belief win:
  n_obstacle = 6 (~3× other corners, experiment.py:686-694) exists specifically
  so the wall∘optimize policy holds a stitch slot and belief stays shallow; and
  the belief_scaffold tasks (experiment.py:756-764) are plain-belief tasks
  injected to seed the fork(policy, sync) abstraction. Neither bakes in a mental
  primitive — they tune which abstractions form, and the scaffold is excluded
  from the verdict — but a reviewer can say the corpus was shaped until the
  desired abstraction won. Both need to be in the paper's limitations/disclosure
  text (the code comments already contain the right arguments), ideally with a
  sensitivity note: what happens at n_obstacle = 4, and with --no-curriculum.

  4. Small asymmetries worth one sentence each. content_q's wall-coordinate
  boost (experiment.py:191-209) is family-aware in effect — it fires only for
  real, rendered walls, and the comment proves belief's phantom wall can't
  trigger it; that proof belongs in the thesis, since a reviewer will check
  whether the coordinate prior leaks into the belief search. And simplify
  (dsl.py:1039-1073) is the one hand-written rewrite in the codebase, and it
  targets exactly fork/sync nesting; it's semantics-preserving and only strips a
  redundant wrapper enumeration artifacts produce, but since it canonicalizes
  belief-shaped trees specifically before length comparison and stitching, say
  so and say why it can't shorten the minimal belief form relative to rivals.

  What I'd do

  1. Report a full --no-dream run next to the headline run — this single
  ablation neutralizes the entire Q objection.
  2. In the thesis, reframe the encoder as an extensional perceptual front-end
  and enumerate its guarantees (uniform floor, fallback rounds, Q-independent
  pricing); consider the generic-encoder variant if you have compute.
  3. Replace "non-mental basis" with "extensional basis" where precision
  matters, and handle optimize head-on.
  4. Move the code-comment disclosures (obstacle density, scaffold, wall-coord
  boost, degenerate scope-complement commits) into the thesis's own disclosure
  section — they're currently your best answers to the referee, hidden in
  source.

  The deepest structural worries — fork/sync being a disguised believe
  primitive, the privileged channel direction, the fixed arity — are the ones
  your design already answers well (independent extension in file14-style
  families, the cube complements with via_swap, phase 2's decomposition, phase
  3's free arity). I found nothing in the interpreter or primitive semantics
  that smuggles in a representation-of-a-representation.
