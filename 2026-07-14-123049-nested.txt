  - Goal-displacement is quietly degrading too, and this matters more than it
  looks: phase 1 solves 24/24, but 10 of those are wall-edits in the private
  model; phase 2 solves only 15/24 and all 15 are wall-edits — the genuine
  goal-edit spellings (optimize (distance gv) gv or step gv down as the derive
  step) vanished entirely under the longer decomposed spelling.

  Q1: Drop the duals?

  No — but don't keep them as dead weight either. They carry two things nothing
  else carries: they're the only family that kills the interleaved
  transient-wall rival (_seq(W1,o1,C1,W2,o2,C2)), and a solved dual is your only
  demonstration that the constructor composes — two believers = two
  applications, which is the productivity sentence an examiner will want. Since
  a prior run found them in exactly that form, the fix is search, not task
  design: the round-4 uniform-Q fallback your log already flagged (dreamed Q was
  still producing solves when uniform stalled), a raised --t-fn for late rounds
  only, or a small dual-scaffold curriculum analogous to belief_scaffold (two
  agents, non-contradictory walls — shallow, seeds the
  compose-of-two-constructors shape). Only if they stay at 0 after that should
  you cut them — and then cut the transient-schedule-rival claim with them, and
  lean on mdl_margin.py's pricing of the ground-truth dual spelling as the
  honest fallback ("the concept makes it cheap; search variance missed it").

  Q2: Make the tasks more general?

  Yes, but the gap isn't task variety — it's that the derive-step hole is
  collapsing to wall_at. If nearly every belief solve puts a wall in the hole
  (phase 2 is already there), the learned abstraction is "phantom-wall belief,"
  not belief, and the goal-displacement family stops doing its job of killing
  wall-shaped accounts. Two cheap, targeted fixes beat inventing new families:

  1. Tighten the goal-displacement generator: add a model-wall rival to its
  rejection battery — discard any scene where some single wall_at edit inside 
  the private model reproduces the trajectory. Your existing _wall_explainable
  only checks world-walls. This forces the hole to be a goal-edit, restoring
  content diversity by construction.
  2. Check why phase 2 lost the goal-edit spellings — they're one or two tokens
  longer under the decomposed constructor, so this is likely a budget cliff, and
  it interacts with the same late-round Q lever as the duals.

  Q3: Richer belief contents — other agents' functions, nested beliefs?

  Beliefs about another agent's function: you mostly get this for free, without 
  new machinery. In your DSL an agent's policy is optimize(neg_dist gv, av) —
  it's parameterized entirely by grid contents. So "agent 1 has a false belief
  about agent 2's goal/policy" is just a private model whose gv marker is
  misplaced, which the current grid-valued models already express. You can build
  those tasks today; the derive step becomes a move-the-other-agent's-goal
  edit. Genuinely function-valued belief contents (the model stores a lambda)
  would need a new type in the interpreter and would break the fork/sync story —
  I'd leave that as explicitly future work.

  Nested beliefs are the one ambitious extension worth real consideration, and
  phase 3 is where they belong: the cons/nil grid-stack already makes
  model-depth a free parameter, and right now phase 3's verdict is a null result
  ("MDL never selects >1 channel"). Tasks whose cheapest explanation requires a
  model-within-a-model (agent 1 detours because it predicts agent 2's
  wall-driven detour) would upgrade that to a dose-response: MDL selects exactly
  the nesting depth the data demands — a much stronger "the frame is
  discovered, not stipulated" claim. But sequence it honestly: nested-belief
  programs are at least as deep as duals, and duals are currently at 0/24. Fix
  the dual-search levers first; if the system can't find a depth-2 compose, it
  won't find a depth-2 nesting. And budget for the rival battery being genuinely
  hard there — you have to exclude every first-order private-model reading,
  which is a bigger space than any battery you've written so far.

  Priority order I'd recommend: (1) late-round dreamed-Q / t_fn fix and re-run —
  it plausibly rescues duals and the phase-2 goal-displacement regression at
  once; (2) the model-wall rejection in the goal-displacement generator; (3)
  belief-about-other's-goal tasks as the cheap "belief about an agent, not a
  wall" content extension; (4) nested beliefs in phase 3 if, and only if, (1)
  lands.
