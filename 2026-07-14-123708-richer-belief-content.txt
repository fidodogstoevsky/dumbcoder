⏺ I've now got the structural picture. The key fact is in dsl.py:205-233 and the
  type declarations: belief content isn't a proposition in your model — it's a 
  grid. The agent's private model is a second numpy array, co-typed with the
  world, and a "belief" like (wall_at r c) is just a counterfactual grid that
  differs from the actual world by a 3 at one cell. fork derives that grid;
  sync_to_world reconciles it by diffing two grids.

  That representational choice is what determines your answer. It's not one
  question — "more complicated content" splits into three very different asks:

  1. Richer single-world content — basically free

  Anything expressible as "the world is like this grid instead of the actual
  one" already works. Your content language is exactly the set of fn = grid -> 
  grid programs that build the private channel (wall_at, optimize, step,
  compositions). Want "the agent believes the goal moved" or "believes a wall
  and a different agent position"? That's just a richer derive. No interpreter
  change — you're already there, you'd just author/search harder derive
  programs. Any first-order spatial configuration is in scope today.

  2. Nested belief (A believes B believes P) — extension, not overhaul, if you 
  do it the right way

  Here's the leverage: fork is already type-agnostic. Look at it —

  def _f(w):
      return commit((w.copy(), derive(w.copy())))

  It only assumes the state has .copy(). The grid-specificity lives entirely in
  the leaves (sync_to_world's argwhere, wall_at's gset). So nesting does not
  require new belief machinery. It requires making the world type recursive — a
  world whose cells/regions can themselves embed a modeled world. Then "A
  believes B believes P" is just: A's private model is a world that contains 
  B-holding-a-private-model-of-P. The same fork/derive/commit re-applies at each
  level.

  This is the move that's actually in the spirit of file17. The gstack made
  channel arity (how many models held at once) a recursive free parameter.
  Nesting is the orthogonal recursion: make content depth (a model whose content
  is itself a modeled world) recursive. Cost is real but bounded — you'd lift
  sync_to_world/overlay to the recursive type, or keep grids but let a region
  encode a sub-state. The interpreter core doesn't change. And critically, it
  preserves your thesis: nesting falls out of a richer state type, discovered by
  composition, not from a belief primitive.

  3. Propositional content — disjunction, negation, quantification, modality — 
  genuinely new axis

  This is the one that's a fundamental change, and there are two ways to do it,
  one of which is a trap:

  - The trap (overhaul and self-defeating): introduce a symbolic proposition
  language with And/Or/Not/Believes(agent, P) constructors. A Believes former is
  intensional by construction — it's precisely the disqualifying move your
  synthesis-not-primitive memory records ("re-buries the file6–9 mistake in the
  DSL and leaves stitch nothing to discover"). You'd be hardcoding the agent
  structure into the proposition language.
  - The honest extensional version: disjunction/negation aren't representable as
  a single counterfactual grid — "wall at A or wall at B" is two worlds, not
  one. So the private channel would have to become a set of grids (possible
  worlds). Note this is a different axis from file17's arity: arity is multiple
  grids held simultaneously/conjunctively; a possible-worlds set is multiple
  grids held alternatively/disjunctively. Your cube (role) and stack
  (conjunctive arity) don't cover it. This is a real new combinator family
  (powerset of states + the right commit semantics), but it stays extensional
  and keeps things discoverable.

  Bottom line
