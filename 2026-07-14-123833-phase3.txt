⏺ Answer: the cube is still strictly binary — two grids, exactly. It introduces
  symmetry along the choice axes within a fixed 2-grid product; it does not
  generalize the arity of the tuple.

  Here's the precise situation in the code:

  The state shape is monomorphic and capped at two channels. dsl.py:280-287
  declares exactly two state shapes: grid (no private channel) and pair_gg = 
  (grid, grid) (one private channel). There is no triple_ggg, no n-ary product
  type, and the comment is explicit that these are "two monomorphic state
  shapes." The arity is baked into the type, not a parameter.

  What the cube actually generalizes over is the choice baked into each 
  combinator, not the number of grids. Every axis in the census (dsl.py:429-436)
  is a symmetry inside the binary product:
  - direction: which of the two channels you read vs. write (sync_to_world ↔
  sync_to_model)
  - z-order / projection: which of the two wins ties / survives
  (overlay↔underlay, fst_gg↔snd_gg)
  - bifunctor: mapsnd↔mapfst↔bimap — "second," "first," "both" of a pair
  - pairing: dup↔pair_blank — diagonal vs. fresh, but both produce a pair_gg
  - via_swap/swap (dsl.py:529-535): the symmetry witness literally exchanges the
  two channels

  Every one of these is an operation on pair_gg. swap exchanging "the two
  channels" only typechecks because there are exactly two. The product-category
  decomposition (dup/mapsnd/compose_gp/pipe_gpg, dsl.py:308-350) is the binary
  product ×, and the comment at dsl.py:324 notes the type system has "no generic
  compose across grid/pair arrows" — it's hand-wired for one extra channel.

  So the symmetry argument it buys you is: "among all the ways to wire two
  channels together, joint MDL still picks the read-model/write-world/single-av
  corner for belief." That defends against the charge that sync_to_world was a
  gerrymandered primitive. It does not defend against the charge that the 2-grid
  frame itself (one world + one model) was hand-picked. Arity is the one degree
  of freedom the cube holds fixed.

  If you want the stronger claim — that belief discovers it needs exactly one
  private channel rather than zero, three, or four — you'd need a genuinely
  different generalization: an n-ary tuple type (or a cons/nil channel-stack) so
  that "one model grid" competes against "two scratch grids," "no private
  channel," etc., in the same search. That's a different symmetry (arity
  symmetry) than the one the cube currently introduces (channel-role symmetry).
  Right now the monomorphic pair_gg type forecloses it by construction.

  One thing worth confirming on your end: whether you want arity-generalization,
  given the [[synthesis-not-primitive]] thesis. There's a defensible position
  that "two grids = world + model" is the irreducible setup of a theory-of-mind
  task and shouldn't be up for search — in which case fixing arity at 2 is
  principled, not a gap. But if a reviewer presses "why two and not three," the
  cube as written has no answer; only the binary-product type does, and that's
  an interpreter commitment, not a discovered fact.

⏺ All three run. Now the real answer: your two scenarios live on different axes,
  and only one is what arity buys you. There are actually three orthogonal
  generalizations of belief here, and it's worth keeping them apart.

  The three axes

  1. Arity = breadth (this is what file17 added): how many models you hold side 
  by side.
  This is the right axis for multiple agents reasoned about jointly — A weighing
  B's and C's predicted moves at the same instant, collision resolution, "act
  on the gap between two minds." Construction (3) above does this: two agents'
  policies run on two derived models held live, then a zip_top combines them →
  genuine arity-2.

  But note the catch from the serialization finding: multiple agents with 
  independent beliefs do not need arity. Construction (1) — two agents,
  different phantom walls, different goals — is just compose(fork(p₁, sync a₁), 
  fork(p₂, sync a₂)). Each model is derived, used, and collapsed before the
  next; max 2 grids live; arity-1, and expressible with plain atomic fork, no
  stack. You only climb to arity-2 when the agents' models must be combined 
  before either commits (interaction), not when they merely coexist in the
  scene.

  2. Nesting depth = recursion: models of models.
  This is the axis for recursive mind-reading — "A believes that B believes the
  wall is at X." And here's the thing: arity has nothing to do with it, and it 
  was already expressible before file17. It's fork in the derive position of
  another fork. Construction (2): fork(fork(p_B, sync_B), p_A→sync_A) — A's
  private model is the world after B has acted on B's belief. Each extra "I
  think that you think…" is one more fork nesting, unbounded, and it stays
  arity-1 because each level collapses to a single grid before the next reasons
  about it (B's full model isn't held while A thinks — only B's result).

  So recursive ToM is depth, multi-agent-at-once is breadth, and the grid-stack
  generalized breadth.

  3. Persistence = time: models that survive across frames.
  Neither axis above touches this, and it's the one your scenarios may secretly
  want. Right now every private channel is within-step ephemeral — belief is
  rederived from scratch each frame (memoryless). An agent whose belief updates 
  over time (B sees something at t, remembers it at t+1, revises) needs a
  channel that persists across the unfold loop. That's the machine_gg
  persistent-state architecture (mk_machine_gg in dsl.py), which file17
  deliberately left alone. False-belief tasks in the deepest sense — B's belief
  diverges from reality because of when B last looked — live here.

  So, directly

  - Multiple agents with different beliefs? Yes — and trivially (sequential
  composition, arity-1, no new machinery). The arity work only earns its keep
  when the agents interact (joint move computed from several live models).
  - Recursively mind-reading each other? Yes — but via nesting, which the
  single-grid fork calculus already had; file17's arbitrary channels don't
  provide it. ("Each other" mutually — A models B modeling A — is just deeper
  nesting, with a fixpoint/termination question if it's truly circular.)
  - The honest gap: sustained, updating beliefs over time (memory, belief
  revision) need axis 3, which isn't built.

  One caveat that ties back to the last finding: even these multi-agent and ToM
  constructions tend to serialize unless there's a genuine simultaneous combine
  — so most of them will be MDL-selected at arity-1, just like cross-blur. The
  stack makes the breadth expressible; whether a task forces it is the same
  question as before.
