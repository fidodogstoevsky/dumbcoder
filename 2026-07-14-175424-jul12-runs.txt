  Note the confound before drawing conclusions about t-fn: the short and long 
  pairs ran different code. The short runs (jobs 24573390/92) were generated
  without the relocate family (283 tasks); the long runs (24573602/03) include
  it (299). So "long run failed" is really "long timeout + 6 rounds + relocate
  corpus failed" — though the failure mechanisms below point at the timeout, not
  relocate (relocate itself behaved exactly as designed: 16/16 solved,
  clear_at∘wall_at, bare prims, claimed no belief abstraction).

  Why phase1 long fails where phase1 short passes

  In the short run, round 1 (1200s) only reaches the cheap solutions; the
  wall-belief tasks (fork∘wall∘optimize∘sync at ~103k enumeration) are out of
  reach and get solved in round 2 through the invented fn_1 policy. That makes
  the fused pattern (fork (fn_1 …) (sync_to_world …)) massively frequent, so
  stitch fuses it into fn_11 — the AGENT TYPE CONSTRUCTOR with the shared $3
  hole (actor AND committer), which is exactly the (B) verdict.

  In the long run, 3600s lets round 1 catch the wall-beliefs raw (at 102k–104k),
  plus two kinds of pollution:

  1. Step-down derives: (fork (compose (step X down) (optimize …)) 
  (sync_to_world …)) at ~77–79k — legitimate goal-displacement solutions, but
  they push stitch to invent fn_11 = (fn_0 #1 #0 (step #0 down)), classified
  "desire fragment". With wall-beliefs already solved raw, stitch factors seek
  (fn_6) and wall-policy (fn_7) as separate abstractions and the fused wall
  constructor never pays for itself → "constructor invented: False".
  2. A genuine non-mental rival: line 93, (compose (compose (step 9 right) 
  (optimize (distance 9) 9)) (optimize (distance 4) 9)) at 189730 — a
  pure-compose, no-fork/no-sync solution to a belief task. This is almost
  certainly what flips "wall-belief solutions are wall-based" to False. Notably
  the same program appears in both phase2 runs at only 25110 (the decomposed DSL
  reorders enumeration), so this scene leaks under every config that searches
  far enough — it's a task-construction hole, not a search artifact.

  The cube census for belief also collapses to 9 tokens (distance×3, neg_dist×3,
  sync_to_world×3, no wall_at) versus the short run's 18 — worth checking
  whether that census counts surface tokens of rewritten programs only; if
  wall_at buried inside fn_7/fn_9 bodies doesn't count, the check gets
  structurally harder to pass the deeper stitch rewrites, which would make the
  cube criterion budget-dependent for the wrong reason.

  What I'd take from this

  The thesis-friendly story is not "short runs pass because we searched less."
  It's that the current corpora still contain scenes where non-mental or
  degenerate programs are extensionally correct, and a bigger budget finds them.
  The verdict should be robust to search budget; right now it's anti-monotone
  in compute, which a reviewer will spot. Concrete patches, in order of value:
  (a) kill the pure-compose rival scene (the 189730/25110 program — identify
  which belief task it solves and diversify it like you did the beacon leak),
  (b) make snd_gg-readout fail on all belief scenes (output must differ from the
  final model channel), (c) block the erase-value rival on false-obstacle, (d)
  fix the degenerate/forced-commit classifier, (e) then re-run long — if the
  verdict holds at 3600s the discovery claim is much stronger than the current
  1200s pass.
